## Source inspection

2 images (8192² atlas shared by buildings and trees, 1024² for figures), 4 materials, 17 meshes. Everything is in one
scene: a stone-paved square with a temple, rotunda, houses, market stalls and a fountain on a thin plinth, and a canal along
the south edge (water, two quay walls, a bridge, boats, far bank). Main mesh is 14 chunks, 340k triangles.

Cut from the source:
- "Fire" mesh (alpha-blended flames) and "Attachments" (small figures, separate texture): skipped by material.
- Canal water, bridge, boats and the far bank: the scene is clipped at the source Z plane of the north quay edge
  (`cutSrcZ: 0.269`), straddling triangles are split (Sutherland-Hodgman), so the model ends in a clean quay wall.
- Faces that look down (underside of the plinth, eaves) are dropped; the camera never sees them.
- The fountain basin is kept (small, part of the square). Trees and the plinth are kept.

## Importer

`scripts/import-landmarks.mjs` (deterministic, `--check` covers it; `--only=<id>` is a new dev aid) and the new
`scripts/lib/atlas-bake.mjs`. The usual UV-aware simplification does not work here: at 30:1 on a patchwork 8192² atlas it
smears unrelated texels (the D038 failure; first attempts were garbled). So `bake: true`:
1. weld by position, simplify position-only with meshoptimizer (`Prune`), 10,758 triangles;
2. unwrap the proxy into planar charts (connected, within about 30 degrees), shelf-pack at uniform density in 1024²;
3. bake each texel by casting a ray from the proxy surface onto the original scene (BVH) and sampling the
   box-downscaled source texture; dilate gutters.

Existing palace, castle and culture are byte-identical (their GLBs show no diff; `--check` passes). Import time for rome is
about 15-20 s, the whole set about 62 s (the vitest allows 120 s).

Output: 1 part, 10,758 triangles (cap 11,000), JPEG atlas 1024² (q85), GLB 945,256 bytes (byte budget for this model raised to
1 MB; the other landmarks keep 700 KB), footprint check passes (18.4 ≤ 20, 16.5 ≤ 18). Facing: the temple faces -Z after
`rotationY: PI`, like the other landmarks. No tone change was needed (baked shadows read fine under the game light, no clipping:
the texture is a straight resample).

Night: no window lights. The atlas has no identifiable window layer and landmarks have no `_window` attribute; same as the others.
Not done.

## Known limits

- The south quay face shows a thin teal line at the foot of the wall (water colour baked there).
- Texture density is about 26 px/m (1024² for 760 m² of surface); fine at game zoom, soft up close.
- The 10×9 footprint is large; on the city map it needs a free flat area (the QA finds one east of the culture house).
